# T-138: Night readability 1: lighter night tint, warm light pools, red Final Horde (R-9; absorbs T-099)

Milestone: M6 | Type: code | Owner: gemini | Scenario: `night`

## Goal

Owner request **R-9**: at night the pixel zombies are near-black silhouettes on a dark blue world and can't be told
apart from bushes (see `project/runs/T-106/a1/qa_qa_m3_breach.png`). Keep the night mood, but make the base and the
threats readable. This is the **world-lighting half**. T-139 (next) does the zombie-specific half (outline, eyes,
HP bars). This task also absorbs **T-099**: the night-20 Final Horde must look clearly red.

## Context

- `game/scripts/world/day_night_tint.gd` (`DayNightTint extends CanvasModulate`, node `World/DayNightTint` in
  `game/scenes/world/world.tscn`, bound from `game/scripts/main.gd:19`). `NIGHT_COLOR = (0.42, 0.48, 0.72)`,
  `FINAL_COLOR = (0.62, 0.34, 0.36)`. `tint_for()` is a pure static function tested by `game/tests/test_day_night.gd`.
- T-123 made the ground light (#c-ish olive) and the paths dark, so the ground is no longer the problem. The problem
  is that the tint multiplies the dark zombie/bush palette down to near black.
- Buildings come from `BuildingStore` signals (`building_added/removed/completed`); positions are tile-based on the
  64 px grid. The HUD and menus are on CanvasLayers and are not tinted.

## Do

1. **Lighter, less saturated night.** Raise `NIGHT_COLOR` so mid-tones survive (start around `(0.55, 0.60, 0.80)`
   and judge by capture). Dusk and dawn ramps stay as they are.
2. **Red Final Horde (T-099).** `FINAL_COLOR` must read clearly red/ominous next to a normal night, e.g.
   `(0.80, 0.42, 0.42)`, without crushing units to black. Keep `tint_for()` pure and static.
3. **Warm light pools.** New `game/scripts/world/night_lights.gd` (`class_name NightLights extends Node2D`), a child
   of `World` added in `world.tscn` and bound from `main.gd` next to `DayNightTint`. For each **completed** Command
   Center, Shelter, Watchtower, Turret and Radio Tower, place one `PointLight2D` (warm colour ~`#ffcf8a`, soft radial
   gradient texture built once in code with `GradientTexture2D`, shared by all lights) centred on the building.
   Radius about 3 tiles for the CC, 2 for the others. `energy` follows the night: 0 by day, ramps up over dusk and
   down over dawn (derive it from the same phase/elapsed/time_left inputs, as a pure static
   `NightLights.energy_for(phase, elapsed, time_left, day) -> float` so it can be unit-tested). Lights are pooled
   by building uid; removed or destroyed buildings drop their light. No light for blueprints/under construction.
4. Don't touch zombie, survivor or HP-bar code (T-139 owns those). Don't change sim code.
5. **Captures** (1280×720, 1×) into `project/runs/T-138/a#/`: `night` (night 1), `final_night` (night 20),
   `qa_m3_breach`, and `default` (day, to prove day is unchanged). Put before/after pairs for night and final_night
   (before = HEAD) in one contact sheet `night_before_after.png`.

## Acceptance criteria

- `night` capture: the base glows warm, the ground and props keep visible detail, and zombies are visibly lighter
  than in the T-106 capture (not near-black).
- `final_night` capture is clearly red next to `night`; units and buildings are still readable.
- Day capture unchanged (tint is white by day; light energy 0).
- `test_day_night.gd` updated for the new constants; new `test_night_lights.gd`: energy 0 by day, 1 at night,
  monotonic over dusk; a light is created for a completed CC, none for a blueprint, and it is freed when the building
  is removed.
- All tests pass; import, smoke and screenshot clean. Final Horde with 150+ zombies (`zombies_perf`) shows no frame
  drop from the lights (lights are per building, not per unit).

## Pitfalls

- `PointLight2D` only lights items whose `light_mask` matches its `range_item_cull_mask`; defaults (1/1) are fine.
  Lights add on top of the CanvasModulate, which is what we want.
- Keep the gradient texture small (e.g. 128×128) and shared. Use `texture_scale` for radius, don't build one texture
  per light.
- Pixel-art rule: lights may be smooth (they are light, not sprites), but never scale or blur sprites.
- Don't put lights under the `YSort` node; they should not be y-sorted. A sibling of `YSort` under `World` is fine.
- The orchestrator's default screenshot for this task is the `night` scenario: make sure it shows the base.
